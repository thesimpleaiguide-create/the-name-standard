What is an intimate examination?

It can include examination of the breasts, genitalia, or rectum. It can also include other contact or observation that requires close physical proximity, exposure, or could reasonably make a patient feel vulnerable or uncomfortable.

Why chaperoning matters

A chaperone is there to safeguard the patient and clinician, support dignity, provide reassurance, act as an impartial observer, and raise concerns where needed.

Remote consultations count

Chaperone considerations apply to video and remote consultations where sensitive discussion, visual examination, image sharing, exposure, or safeguarding risks arise.

Patient safety is everyone's role

Reception and administrative colleagues may be the first people to identify a need for a chaperone, communicate available options, record a request, or arrange a suitable appointment.

The policy connection

Local chaperoning arrangements should align with current NHS expectations, safeguarding practice, sexual-safety commitments, equality duties, and clear escalation processes.
